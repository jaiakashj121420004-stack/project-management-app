01 — Executive Summary

Where Aurora stands, in one page

What this report answers, and the honest short version of each answer.

Aurora (by Nvexis) is a multi-tenant, installable Progressive Web App for project management — Kanban boards, a daily to-do planner, a unified calendar, a Notion-style notes editor, and a real-time collaborative whiteboard canvas, plus a growing set of team features (goals, fixed-rule automations, time tracking, file attachments, a client-facing share link, project templates, one-time Trello/CSV import, an MCP server that connects Claude Desktop/Code directly to a user's account, and funnel analytics). It runs on React 19 + TypeScript + Vite on the frontend, Supabase (Postgres + Auth + Realtime + Storage + Edge Functions) on the backend, Cloudflare Pages for hosting, and Dodo Payments as Merchant of Record. It is built and operated, day to day, by essentially one person working with AI coding sessions under a documented workflow (CLAUDE.md), with 56 database migrations and 13 Edge Functions shipped between 18 June and 22 August 2026 — roughly nine weeks.

This report answers three questions plainly, and does not round any of the answers up:

  • Is the product good? Yes, and unusually broad for its team size — see §3–4. It also carries real, named weaknesses (a handful of shipped-but-not-yet-Windows-verified features, one built feature that is not yet wired live, a genuine solo-maintainer bus-factor risk) that this report does not paper over — see §9.
  • Who's the competition, and is there a real edge? Trello, Asana, ClickUp, Monday.com, Notion, and Todoist all compete for the same buyer to varying degrees. Aurora's most defensible, quantifiable edge — flat per-board pricing instead of per-seat — is unchanged and, per fresh research this session, still not matched by any of them. See §8.
  • What will it realistically earn, and how will it be marketed? A credible one-year target for a disciplined solo operator running organic channels well is low-thousands of dollars in ARR, not a hockey stick — see §13's "honest expectations." The marketing plan in §14 is deliberately free/organic-first and gates any paid spend on real, Dodo-confirmed revenue, not a calendar date.

What changed since the 13 August 2026 edition, in one paragraph: a large amount of real product surface shipped in the intervening nine days — goals, automations, time tracking, file attachments, full-text search, project templates with a custom template builder, one-time Trello/CSV import, recurring Kanban cards, a client-facing read-only share link, the MCP connector, a Calendar Timeline/Gantt view and a Day view, table blocks, math formulas, sub/superscript, note export to Markdown/Word/PDF, a funnel-analytics layer that plainly did not exist on 13 August, a written bus-factor runbook, a scoped-and-budgeted pentest plan, and a formalized Simplicity Guardrail review process that has already overruled itself once in public (see §8). This report re-verifies every material figure from the 13 August edition rather than republishing it, and updates the ones that moved.

03 — Product Overview: Every Feature, In Detail

What Aurora actually does, area by area

Built by reading src/features/* directly, folder by folder, plus memory.md's Feature log — not a highlight reel.

Kanban boards

Columns with drag-and-drop cards (dnd-kit); checklists; labels; a priority tier per card; due dates, with due time (not just date) on Pro; assignees; recurring cards on a schedule (built 17 August 2026 by wiring the to-do planner's existing recurrence engine to boards); file attachments (16 August 2026, reusing the same private-Storage-bucket-plus-signed-URL pattern the notes/canvas media already used); per-card time tracking with start/stop and a running total, aware of multiple users on the same card and enforcing one active timer per person account-wide via a partial unique index (16 August 2026); threaded comments with @mentions and reactions; a review/approval workflow; a per-card activity feed; and card-level reminders, with Pro getting custom offsets ("2h before", "15m before") and multiple channels. Why it matters: this is the part of Aurora a first-time user touches first, and it is deliberately the one place every other feature (notes, time, files, discussion) surfaces without leaving the board.

To-Do planner

A personal daily/weekly planner, separate from project boards, with custom recurrence rules (daily/weekly/monthly/interval), priority tiers, bulk actions, swipe gestures on mobile, drag-and-drop reordering with fractional positioning and an auto-sink-on-complete behavior, and starter templates so a new user has something useful the moment they open it rather than a blank list. Why it matters: not every task belongs on a shared board — this is where personal, non-project work lives, and it's the thing Aurora's own Simplicity Audit calls out as already doing item 3 of the guardrail (zero-config default) correctly.

Calendar

Month, Week, Day (added 22 August 2026), and a Timeline/Gantt view (added 20 August 2026) — all reading from the same unified data (cards, to-dos, and project milestones) rather than four separate systems. A project color-key legend appears once more than one project is in view. An ICS subscribe feed (Pro-gated) lets Google/Apple/Outlook calendars see Aurora's dates one-way. Why it matters: this is the feature reviewers of every competitor studied in §8 call out as a bolt-on when it exists at all; Aurora's version is a first-class, unified surface, not an afterthought.

Notes

A Notion-style block/rich-text editor built on Tiptap: headings, bullet/ordered/task lists, tables (with per-column resize), image upload and clipboard paste, embeds, math formulas (KaTeX, inline and block, click-to-edit with a live preview), subscript and superscript, an auto-generated clickable table of contents per note (22 August 2026, with anchors that lazily backfill onto notes written before the feature existed), folders, templates (both curated system templates and a "save as template" custom builder), full-text search, and export to Markdown, Word (.docx, Pro), and PDF (Pro). Drag-and-drop block reordering exists in code but is not yet live (see §2). Why it matters: for the freelancer/consultant persona in §5, this is what replaces a separate docs tool.

Canvas

An infinite, real-time collaborative whiteboard (Konva for rendering, Yjs for the CRDT layer, perfect-freehand for pressure-sensitive strokes): freehand drawing, text boxes, images and media (audio/video, in-app recording, upload, or embedding YouTube/Vimeo/Loom/SoundCloud), tables, pages/frames, z-ordering, multi-select, a layers panel, and undo/redo. Live multiplayer cursors and presence. Why it matters: most competitors in §8 either bolt this on or omit it; Aurora ships it as a native, Pro-gated primitive.

Collaboration

Owner/editor/viewer roles enforced in Postgres Row Level Security, not just hidden buttons; email-keyed invitations (so an invitee without an account yet can still be invited); live presence; real-time sync; a client-facing, no-account-required read-only share link for boards (shipped 16 August 2026, the direct answer to a documented freelancer need — showing a client progress without giving them a seat); and standalone note/personal-canvas sharing with its own role model, audited and bug-fixed for real on 22 August 2026 (three UI call sites were silently ignoring the recipient's actual role — the underlying RLS was correct throughout, only the UI's interpretation of it was wrong).

Goals

Lightweight, flat progress-bar goals — deliberately not an OKR system with an Objectives/Key-Results split. This was a conscious Simplicity Guardrail decision, shipped free rather than Pro-gated because the mitigation is scope, not a paywall (see §8/§9's discussion of the guardrail overruling its own advisory flag with a written reason).

Automations

A small, fixed trigger/action rule set, Pro/Team-gated — deliberately not a general-purpose "if X then Y" rule builder, which the guardrail's own research names as the single most direct path to the "feature overload" that competitor reviewers cite as ClickUp's top churn driver.

Import

One-time Trello JSON export / CSV import into a new Aurora project — one-way, no ongoing sync, aimed squarely at lowering the switching cost from an existing tool.

Search & command palette

Full-text search across card and note content (added 17 August 2026 — previously the command palette only indexed names, not content), surfaced inside the same ⌘K command palette used for quick navigation across projects, notes, canvases, and folders — one familiar affordance extended, not a second search UI.

MCP server ("Connect Claude")

A genuinely unusual feature for a project-management tool at this stage: a Pro+ user can connect Claude Desktop or Claude Code directly to their own Aurora account via a personal access token, and read or write boards, to-dos, and notes through the same RLS-enforced API surface the app itself uses. The token is generated once, shown once, and only its SHA-256 hash is ever stored; a database trigger blocks any writer to that column other than the service role. Each tool call is authenticated by a short-lived (5-minute) JWT minted from a dedicated, independently-revocable signing key — not the project's normal session-signing key — so a compromise of one can't forge the other. Why it matters: it lets a technical user (see the fourth marketing angle in §14) manage their boards from inside an AI coding session instead of a browser tab, and it's real, novel authentication infrastructure the pentest brief (§10) flags as its highest-priority area to test.

Personalization

Day/Night themes, font pairing, and custom color presets — an 8-tile curated grid leading, with a raw hex/color-wheel picker demoted to a collapsed "Advanced" disclosure (a deliberate guardrail-driven rebuild after an earlier raw-picker version produced ugly, uncoordinated results) — synced to the account, not just the device, since 15 August 2026.

PWA / installability

Installable on mobile and desktop from any browser, offline caching via a service worker, and platform-appropriate install-prompt handling (a real native prompt on Chrome/Edge/Android, a guided "Add to Home Screen" walkthrough on iOS Safari, which exposes no programmatic install API).

Notifications & reminders

Due-date reminders via email (Resend) and browser notification; Pro gets custom timed reminders with configurable lead time and channel.

Billing

Three priced, self-serve tiers (Free/Pro/Team) plus an unpriced, manually-provisioned Enterprise tier for accounts past Team's 40-member cap; Dodo Payments checkout; in-place plan switching; India-localized pricing; and webhook-verified entitlement — the server, never the client, is the source of truth for who is on which plan, enforced by a database trigger that blocks any client write to billing columns.

Analytics (internal)

A minimal, privacy-respecting funnel-tracking layer shipped 15 August 2026 — seventeen allow-listed events, one table, no dashboard, no third-party vendor, no PII fields. Covered in depth in §10 as part of the technology assessment, since its real audience is internal, not end users.

04 — Target User Personas

Who Aurora is actually built for

Grounded in the product's real pricing shape and the features the August improvement pass specifically added — not generic SaaS personas.

Persona 1 — the freelance consultant

Priya, 29, independent brand consultant, Bengaluru

Priya currently runs her practice on a free Trello board, a separate Google Doc per client for notes, and a spreadsheet for time she bills against — three tools, no single source of truth, and a recurring problem: a client asks "where are we?" and she has to assemble an answer by hand across all three. She doesn't want to give a client an account or a login just to see progress. Aurora's client-facing read-only share link (§3, shipped 16 August 2026) solves that directly — she sends a link, the client sees the board's current state, no signup required. Her actual switching trigger to Pro is per-card time tracking: once she's billing against it instead of a separate timer app, going back is real friction. India-priced Dodo checkout (₹399/mo Pro) makes the decision a non-event rather than a currency-conversion sticker shock.

Persona 2 — the small creative/dev team

An 5-person product studio currently on ClickUp

They're paying ClickUp's per-seat Business-tier price for five people and have grown to resent two things: the monthly bill scaling with headcount every time they bring on a contractor, and a toolbar/settings surface that a new hire needs real onboarding time to navigate (exactly the "feature overload" complaint §8's competitor research documents). Aurora's flat per-board pricing — Team at $22/mo covers up to 40 people on a board, not $22 per person — is a direct, quantifiable cost argument the studio's lead can bring to the rest of the team without a spreadsheet. The Simplicity Guardrail's visible discipline (§8, §9) is the qualitative half of the same pitch: a tool that has publicly reasoned about, and rejected, the exact kind of feature bloat they're leaving ClickUp over.

Persona 3 — the price-sensitive student/early-career freelancer

Arjun, 21, computer-science student and part-time freelance developer, Pune

Arjun wants one tool for coursework, a couple of small freelance client projects, and a personal to-do list, but a $10+/seat/month tool is a real budget line for him, not a rounding error. Aurora's ₹-denominated Dodo pricing (Pro at ₹399/mo, roughly $4.60 at the exchange rate this report was written, versus the $5.99 global list price) is the concrete reason he can justify Pro rather than staying on Free indefinitely or not paying at all. He's also the segment most likely to actually use the MCP/Claude connector (§3) — a technically comfortable user who already has Claude Code open for coursework and would rather manage his to-do list from there than switch tabs.

Why the market is growing this fast

  • Remote/hybrid work is now permanent infrastructure, not a pandemic artifact — distributed teams need a shared source of truth a spreadsheet can't provide at scale.
  • Tool consolidation. The fastest-growing sub-segment is "all-in-one" workspaces (Notion, ClickUp, Monday) over single-purpose Kanban tools — Aurora's exact positioning.
  • SMB and solo/freelancer adoption is accelerating faster than enterprise, favoring a low-priced, fast-to-adopt product over an enterprise-first incumbent.
  • AI-adjacent workflow tools are pulling more "planning" software spend into comparison shopping generally.

Where Aurora actually fits

Aurora is not competing for the enterprise PPM buyer (Jira/Asana Enterprise/Monday Enterprise territory — SSO, governance, custom fields at scale). Its real addressable market is the bottom two-thirds of that $10B+ figure: individuals, freelancers, and small teams (2–10 people) currently paying $5–$15/seat/month for some combination of a board tool, a notes tool, and a canvas tool — or paying nothing and limping along with spreadsheets and a free Trello board.

Sizing the realistic slice, unchanged from 13 August: even capturing 0.001% of the ~$10–11B global market implies roughly $100k in category spend redirected toward Aurora — several multiples of even the Best-case revenue scenario in §13. The market is not the constraint; distribution execution is (see §14).

06 — Buyer Psychology

Why people actually pick (and pay for) a tool like this

Carried forward from 13 August where it still checks out, updated for what's genuinely new since then.

Pricing psychology already built into Aurora

  • Anchoring + decoy pricing. Three visible tiers (Free / Pro / Team) let Pro act as the anchored "obvious middle choice" against Team's higher price.
  • Charm pricing. $5.99 and $47.99 (vs. round numbers) reliably outperform round pricing in willingness-to-pay studies.
  • Loss aversion via annual framing. Presenting annual as "33% off" triggers loss aversion more effectively than "save $24/year" framed as a gain.
  • The freemium "foot in the door." A genuinely usable free tier lowers first-use friction to near zero — critical for an unknown indie brand where asking for a card up front would kill most signups.

What actually converts a free user to Pro

Unchanged from 13 August's research (Nielsen Norman Group, OpenView, ProfitWell/Paddle): trigger-moment upgrades beat time-based nags, social proof closes the trust gap for an unknown brand, and users upgrade because one Pro-gated feature becomes indispensable rather than because they compared the whole feature list. What's new since August: the feature set now offers meaningfully more of these "one thing" hooks than it did nine days ago — time tracking, custom reminders, canvas, automations, templates, and exports are all real, distinct reasons a specific user might hit "upgrade." This is a genuine, citable reason to expect Pro-conversion assumptions might land modestly higher than the 13 August estimate — see §13's explicit judgment-call framing, since there is still zero live conversion data to confirm it.

Category-specific psychology: why people switch PM tools at all

Project/task management has unusually high "tool churn" — the two dominant switching triggers are overwhelm (the #1 stated reason people leave Notion and ClickUp) and fragmentation fatigue (juggling 3–4 separate tools). Aurora's calm, all-in-one brand promise answers both directly — and, per §8's fresh competitor research this session, no major competitor has shipped a named "calm mode" or decluttering feature that would blunt this positioning as of 22 August 2026.

08 — SWOT Analysis

Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats

Updated, not copied, from 13 August — grounded in the current codebase and posture. Per explicit founder instruction, the pending pentest and the not-yet-run marketing motion are scheduled pre-launch actions (§11), not listed here as weaknesses or threats.

Strengths

  • Genuinely distinctive, cohesive design system (Fraunces/Spectral/IBM Plex Mono, glass-over-parchment, one-chroma oxblood accent) — few indie tools this age have a real design language
  • Flat per-board pricing undercuts every per-seat competitor for teams of 3+
  • A formalized, actually followed Simplicity Guardrail review process with a real paper trail of self-correction (§7, §9) — a rare, credible anti-bloat discipline
  • Funnel instrumentation now live (shipped 15 August; did not exist in the 13 August edition) — signup→activation→upgrade is measurable, closing the biggest gap the prior report flagged
  • A written solo-maintainer bus-factor runbook now exists (reports/BUS-FACTOR-RUNBOOK.md) — access, redeploy-from-scratch, and fire-drill steps are documented, not only in one person's head
  • Security posture is unusually mature for pre-revenue: RLS on every table, a pgTAP regression suite, a completed 7-phase internal remediation, and a scoped, budgeted pentest ready to commission (§11)
  • Broad, genuinely shipped feature surface (boards + notes + canvas + calendar + time tracking + automations + goals + MCP connector) built by essentially one person
  • Near-zero fixed infrastructure cost at current scale — see §12's cost model
  • India/PPP-aware pricing already built via Dodo, not a future to-do

Weaknesses

  • Solo-maintainer bus-factor risk is real and unresolved even with the runbook — the runbook mitigates the risk (a documented recovery path exists now), it does not remove it (support load, roadmap velocity, and incident response still bottleneck on one person)
  • Funnel analytics has no admin dashboard — it can only be queried directly via SQL in Supabase Studio, a real (if minor) usability gap for anyone other than the person who wrote it
  • Several 22 August 2026 features (note PDF/Word export, the sharing bug-fix, the Contents panel, Calendar Day view) are built and hand-reviewed but not yet through the mandatory Windows build-verification pass (§2) — treat them as unshipped until that gate clears
  • Drag-and-drop block reordering in Notes is built but not wired into the live app (§2, §3) — a concrete example of the "commit/memory says X but the tree doesn't have X" pattern this project's own history has caught more than once
  • Zero brand awareness, zero public reviews, zero social proof — a full "unknown vendor" trust gap with buyers
  • No native mobile app-store presence (App Store / Play Store) — see §15 for the full, re-verified tradeoff

Opportunities

  • Fast-growing category (~13–15% mid-horizon CAGR) with a large, underserved price-sensitive SMB/solo segment
  • "Tool fatigue"/overwhelm remains the #1 stated churn driver from ClickUp/Notion users, and no competitor has shipped an answer to it as of this session's research
  • India and other PPP markets are large, price-driven, and already priced-for in the product
  • The MCP/Claude-connector feature is a genuine, differentiated hook for a technically-forward audience segment that no direct competitor studied here offers (§14's fourth message-testing angle)
  • The flat-per-board pricing story remains a highly shareable, quotable marketing hook, still barely used

Threats

  • Incumbents (Notion, ClickUp, Monday) have vastly larger marketing budgets and could bundle a "calm mode" feature to blunt Aurora's wedge at any time — not observed yet, per this session's research, but the risk itself hasn't gone away
  • Payment-processor/merchant-of-record fee or policy changes (Dodo) directly hit margin given how thin unit economics are pre-scale
  • Solo-founder key-person risk: support load, burnout, or life events could halt the roadmap with no backup, notwithstanding the new runbook

Security model

Row Level Security is enabled on every table, and every per-project table's policy resolves membership through a SECURITY DEFINER helper function rather than a sub-query — the pattern that avoids Postgres's known RLS self-recursion failure mode. A 7-phase internal remediation (REMEDIATION-PLAN.md, closed 14–16 July 2026, verification checklist in PHASE-7-VERIFICATION.md) covered privilege-escalation hardening, SECURITY DEFINER search_path pinning, billing-PII isolation from co-members (a dedicated accessor exposes only name/avatar, not billing columns, to board-mates), and Edge Function hardening (shared sliding-window rate limiting, webhook signature verification, idempotent webhook processing). An automated pgTAP regression suite (supabase/tests/rls_regression.test.sql, 15 assertions) runs in CI on every migration and proves a non-member can't read or write another tenant's data, that a viewer role can read but not write, and that a user can't self-upgrade their own plan column.

A pre-pentest hardening pass on 16 August 2026 re-audited every RLS policy and SECURITY DEFINER function added since the July remediation and found one real issue before handoff to a vendor: two BEFORE INSERT triggers checked a target row's plan/usage before Postgres evaluated the row's own RLS WITH CHECK clause, which let a caller who knew another user's id (visible to board-mates) distinguish "this account is at its plan limit" from "this account has headroom" purely from an insert's error message — a minor information-disclosure bug (an authorization oracle, not a data-write vulnerability; the insert itself always still failed for a non-owner). Fixed in migration 20260816130000_fix_insert_trigger_ownership_oracle.sql, with a new 4-assertion regression test that reproduces the leak against the pre-fix code and confirms it's closed post-fix.

MCP server auth — the newest, least-battle-tested surface

Aurora's own pentest readiness brief (reports/PENTEST-READINESS-2026.md) ranks the MCP connector as the highest-priority area for an external tester, ahead of standard web classes, precisely because it's genuinely novel token-minting infrastructure with no precedent elsewhere in the app. The design: a 32-random-byte token, hashed and stored (never the raw value); a database trigger that rejects any writer to the hash column other than the service role; and a 5-minute-TTL JWT minted per tool call from a dedicated signing key, so that RLS — not custom access-control code in the Edge Function — is what actually decides what a connected Claude session can see or touch.

Analytics endpoint — the first client-writable-by-design surface

analytics_events is the first table in the app where the client is an intentional direct writer rather than an RLS-scoped table consumer — RLS on that table has no policies at all (service-role-only, same stance as the pre-existing webhook/rate-limit tables), so every guarantee lives in the track-event Edge Function: a fixed 17-event allow-list with deliberately vague rejection copy (so the list can't be enumerated by probing), a 4 KB / 30-key cap on scalar-only properties, and the same shared rate limiter the billing functions use. user_id is always stamped server-side from the caller's JWT, never trusted from the request body.

Pentest — scoped, budgeted, and scheduled (not a gap)

Per the founder's explicit framing, this is a planned pre-launch action, covered fully in §11, not listed as a weakness here. The scope (gray-box, ~one app plus its PostgREST/RPC surface, two-to-three tenant accounts across plan tiers) and budget ($4,000–$8,000, per reports/Aurora_Penetration_Test_Options_Report.pdf) are already decided; what remains is commissioning a vendor.

What's genuinely still open, stated plainly

  • Analytics has no admin dashboard — query-only via SQL today.
  • GitHub account MFA is flagged in the bus-factor runbook as an outstanding hardening item and, per a direct check of memory.md this session, remains unresolved.
  • Old, now-rotated secrets (a prior secret-hygiene incident, fully remediated 16 August 2026 — all three exposed credentials rotated same-day) still exist in git history prior to the fix commit. They are inert, so this is hygiene rather than urgent, but a git filter-repo/BFG history scrub is still outstanding.

Free/organic-first, real cadences not just channel names, and paid spend gated on real revenue — not a calendar date.

The core strategic call: organic-first, paid only once customers are actually paying

Aurora now has funnel instrumentation (§9) — the biggest gap the 13 August report flagged is closed. 14 — App Store & Play Store Feasibility

Should Aurora ship to the stores? Re-verified with current facts

Carries forward the 13 August conclusion; re-checked against 2026 policy research this session.

Google Play — technically easy, commercially the real question

Google continues to officially support PWAs on Play via a Trusted Web Activity (TWA) wrapper. The commercial catch is unchanged: a PWA distributed through Google Play that sells digital goods/subscriptions must route those purchases through Google Play Billing — not an external checkout like Dodo's — which would mean maintaining two billing systems and paying Google's cut (typically ~15% for subscriptions after year one) in addition to Dodo's fee, a real margin hit on already-thin unit economics (§12). A narrower middle path — a TWA that links out to the web app, with all billing staying external — can avoid triggering the Play Billing requirement, but 2026 research this session found continued regulatory movement (external-purchase-link allowances tied to antitrust rulings in the US and elsewhere) that keeps this a live, evolving policy area rather than a settled one — consistent with the 13 August report's framing, not resolved since.

Apple App Store — the harder, more expensive path

Unchanged: Apple's Guideline 4.2 requires an app to go meaningfully beyond "a repackaged website," and a bare PWA-in-a-WebView is routinely rejected. iOS still lacks Android/Chrome's native install-prompt affordance — every iOS install remains a manual "Add to Home Screen." A real App Store presence would require a genuine native wrapper (Capacitor, not a raw WebView) with real native functionality, a $99/year developer account, and real engineering time.

Recommendation — unchanged from 13 August

  1. Stay web-only for now. The product already installs from any browser on any platform; app-store presence is a distribution/trust channel, not a requirement. The founder has already confirmed this decision for the current improvement-plan cycle (IMPROVEMENT-PLAN-2026-08.md explicitly skips Play/App Store work on that basis).
  2. Revisit Google Play once there are paying users, as a link-out-only TWA (no in-app billing) purely for Android discovery/trust.
  3. Treat Apple App Store as a Phase 2+ decision, gated on a validated paying user base and engineering slack for a real Capacitor shell.
  4. Do not integrate Google Play Billing unless Android-store distribution proves to be a major acquisition channel and the margin math is re-run at that point.
